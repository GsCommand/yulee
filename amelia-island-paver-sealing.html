Island exposure

Amelia Island paver sealing starts with the surface, not the calendar

Outdoor hardscape on Amelia Island can be exposed to intense sun, humid air, frequent rain, salt-laden coastal conditions, pool water, irrigation and shaded landscaping. Those variables affect how pavers fade, how fast joints lose sand and how long surfaces need to dry before sealing.

We inspect the material and existing coating first. A faded brick-paver driveway with sound joints may need routine maintenance, while a pool deck with low joints, organic staining or a previous incompatible coating can require more preparation before any sealer is applied.

Driveways

Driveway Paver Sealing Amelia Island

UV and heat exposure

Full-sun driveways experience repeated heating and ultraviolet exposure. Over time, color can flatten and prior sealer can wear differently between traffic lanes and protected edges.

Joint-sand loss

Rain, runoff and repeated cleaning can lower joints. We restore depleted sand where the base and drainage are suitable before applying a joint-stabilizing sealer.

Stain treatment

Irrigation, rust, organic residue and tire marks are evaluated before sealing so visible problems are addressed instead of trapped beneath a new finish.

Pool decks and outdoor living

Pool Deck & Patio Paver Sealing Amelia Island

Pool decks, patios and lanais require more attention to moisture and traction than an open driveway. Splash zones, drains, shaded screen enclosures and furniture areas can all influence cleaning, drying and product choice.

Moisture before sealer

Shaded or screened areas can retain moisture long after surrounding pavers appear dry. We verify conditions before application rather than relying only on elapsed time.

Joint loss near drains and edges

Pool drains, cage edges and runoff paths can lose sand faster. Those joints are restored where appropriate after cleaning.

Traction around wet areas

Any wet surface can become slippery. Surface texture, finish, application rate, contamination and maintenance all influence traction after sealing.

Patio borders and transitions

Border courses, steps and connecting walkways are inspected for movement and joint condition so the completed area reads as one finished project.

Natural stone

Travertine Sealing Amelia Island

Travertine and natural stone should not be cleaned or sealed like ordinary concrete pavers. Travertine is calcium-based and can be etched by inappropriate acidic cleaners or damaged by excessive pressure.

Stone-safe cleaning

Cleaning chemistry and pressure are selected for the stone, surface finish and existing products. Open fill and damaged areas are identified before sealing.

Breathable protection

Outdoor stone can absorb moisture through joints, edges and pool splash. A compatible breathable penetrating system is generally preferred after the stone is properly prepared and dry.

Review our detailed travertine sealing process.

Restoration

Paver Cleaning and Resealing Amelia Island

Resealing should begin with the old coating. White haze, cloudy areas, flaking, patchy sheen or heavily worn traffic lanes can signal that more preparation is needed than a standard maintenance coat.

Where the existing system is sound, cleaning, re-sanding and a compatible breathable sealer may be enough. Where the coating has failed, stripping or other corrective preparation may be required before a new finish is applied.
